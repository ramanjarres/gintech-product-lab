# Gintech Business Model

## Purpose

This document describes Gintech's current business model hypothesis.

The model is intentionally designed to evolve as Gintech conducts customer discovery, delivers experiments, validates market demand, and identifies recurring problems that could become scalable products.

Gintech should not assume that the final business model is consulting, SaaS, AI services, or any other predefined category.

The business model should emerge from validated customer problems and demonstrated value.

---

# 1. Core Business Model Hypothesis

Gintech helps organizations identify business problems and opportunities using their operational data, determine what actions could create value, implement appropriate solutions, and measure the resulting impact.

The basic value creation cycle is:

Business Problem
↓
Data
↓
Analysis
↓
Insight
↓
Decision
↓
Action
↓
Measurement
↓
Business Value

Gintech should capture a portion of the value created by making this process possible.

## Stage 1 — Lead

A potential customer enters the Gintech pipeline.

Potential acquisition channels may include:

- Founder network
- Referrals
- LinkedIn
- Direct outreach
- Partnerships
- Workshops and talks
- Content
- Industry communities
- Case studies

At this stage, Gintech should not assume that every lead is a potential customer.

The objective is qualification.

---

## Stage 2 — Discovery

Gintech conducts an initial conversation to understand:

- The organization
- The business model
- The operational context
- Current challenges
- Available data
- Current tools
- Important business metrics
- Existing initiatives
- Decision-making processes

Discovery should be structured and time-bounded.

Its purpose is not to solve the entire problem for free.

Its purpose is to determine whether deeper investigation is justified.

---

## Stage 3 — Opportunity Assessment

Gintech evaluates whether a meaningful opportunity appears to exist.

Key questions include:

1. Is there a real business problem?
2. Is the problem important enough?
3. Can its impact be estimated?
4. Is relevant data available?
5. Can Gintech realistically influence the outcome?
6. Can improvement be measured?
7. Is the potential value greater than the expected cost of intervention?

The output should be an initial:

**Go / No-Go recommendation.**

---

# 3. The Go / No-Go Decision

Not every opportunity should become a project.

### GO

Proceed when there is sufficient evidence that:

- A meaningful problem exists
- The customer cares about solving it
- Relevant data exists or can reasonably be collected
- Gintech has a credible path to influence the outcome
- Success can be measured
- Expected value can justify the investment

### NO-GO

Do not proceed when:

- The problem is unclear
- The business impact is insignificant
- Required data does not exist
- Gintech lacks the required capability
- Another solution is clearly more appropriate
- The customer is not prepared to act on findings
- Expected value does not justify the investment

A No-Go is not necessarily a failed commercial interaction.

It can protect both the customer and Gintech from investing resources in a low-value project.

# 4. Diagnostic

When an opportunity passes initial qualification, Gintech can perform a deeper diagnostic.

Potential areas include:

## Business Diagnostic

Understand:

- Business objectives
- Revenue drivers
- Cost drivers
- Customer behavior
- Operational processes
- Constraints
- Key decisions

## Data Diagnostic

Understand:

- Available datasets
- Data sources
- Data quality
- Missing information
- Data accessibility
- Data relationships
- Measurement limitations

## Opportunity Diagnostic

Identify:

- Performance gaps
- Operational inefficiencies
- Customer friction
- Revenue leakage
- Cost reduction opportunities
- Conversion opportunities
- Retention opportunities
- Automation opportunities

The diagnostic should produce a clear hypothesis about where value may exist.

# 5. Value Hypothesis

Before implementing a solution, Gintech should define a measurable hypothesis.

Example:

> Customers who reduce their usage frequency during a three-week period may have a higher probability of cancelling their membership.

A business hypothesis could then be:

> Identifying these customers earlier and intervening before cancellation may reduce churn.

The important distinction is:

Observation ≠ Value

Insight ≠ Value

Action ≠ Value

Value is created when the action produces a meaningful business outcome.

---

# 6. Pilot

Gintech should prefer small, measurable pilots before large implementations.

A pilot should answer:

> Does this intervention actually create value?

A pilot should define:

- Problem
- Hypothesis
- Data
- Intervention
- Baseline
- Success metric
- Time period
- Expected outcome

Example:

Problem:
Customer churn.

Hypothesis:
Reduced visit frequency predicts churn.

Intervention:
Identify high-risk customers and trigger targeted outreach.

Success metric:
Reduction in churn among the intervention group.

Business metric:
Recovered recurring revenue.

---

# 7. Measurement

Every pilot should attempt to compare:

Before
vs.
After

or, when possible:

Control
vs.
Intervention

Gintech should measure both:

## Operational Metrics

Examples:

- Response time
- Error rate
- Processing time
- Utilization
- Conversion
- Customer engagement

## Business Metrics

Examples:

- Revenue
- Retention
- Cost savings
- Recovered revenue
- Customer lifetime value
- Productivity
- Margin

The purpose is to determine whether the intervention actually created value.

# 9. Potential Revenue Models

Gintech may eventually combine several revenue models.

These are hypotheses, not final decisions.

## A. Diagnostic Engagement

Customer pays for structured analysis of a specific business problem.

Possible output:

- Business analysis
- Data assessment
- Opportunity identification
- Value hypothesis
- Recommended next steps

---

## B. Pilot Project

Fixed-scope engagement designed to validate whether a proposed intervention creates measurable value.

This may become one of Gintech's most important early revenue models.

---

## C. Implementation Project

Gintech implements the validated solution.

Examples:

- Analytics systems
- Data pipelines
- Dashboards
- Automation
- AI workflows
- Decision-support tools

---

## D. Recurring Analytics / Intelligence

Organizations may pay Gintech continuously for:

- Monitoring
- Analysis
- Alerts
- Recommendations
- Performance reviews
- Decision support

Possible commercial model:

Monthly recurring revenue.

---

## E. Productized Service

If Gintech repeatedly solves the same problem using a similar process, the service may become standardized.

Example:

Customer Retention Intelligence

could combine:

Data ingestion
+
Risk detection
+
Customer segmentation
+
Recommended actions
+
Performance measurement

This would be more scalable than fully customized consulting.

---

## F. Software / SaaS

If repeated customer engagements reveal a sufficiently standardized problem, Gintech may eventually build software around it.

The progression should ideally be:

Service
↓
Learning
↓
Repeated Pattern
↓
Standardized Process
↓
Productized Service
↓
Software

Rather than:

Build Software
↓

# 10. Free vs Paid Work

Gintech should clearly separate qualification from value-producing work.

## Potentially Free

- Initial discovery
- Basic qualification
- High-level opportunity assessment
- Determining whether Gintech may be able to help

## Should Generally Be Paid

- Deep data analysis
- Data cleaning
- Root-cause investigation
- Detailed recommendations
- Custom dashboards
- Models
- Automation
- Implementation
- Ongoing monitoring
- Business experimentation

The boundary should be:

> Free work determines whether a valuable engagement exists.

> Paid work creates the value.

# 12. What Gintech Should Avoid

Gintech should avoid becoming dependent on:

- Selling hours without measurable outcomes
- Custom development with no reusable learning
- Generic dashboards
- AI implementations without a business case
- Projects accepted only because the customer is willing to pay
- Unlimited unpaid discovery
- Building software before validating demand

Revenue matters.

But revenue that generates no learning, reusable capability, customer value, or strategic advantage may not be the best revenue for an early-stage Gintech.

# 14. Open Business Model Questions

The following questions remain unresolved:

- Who is the ideal first customer?
- Which industry should Gintech enter first?
- What problem should Gintech solve first?
- How much should the diagnostic cost?
- Should the diagnostic ever be free?
- What should a pilot cost?
- How long should a pilot last?
- Which metrics should determine success?
- Should Gintech charge fixed fees, subscriptions, performance fees, or a combination?
- Which services can become standardized?
- Which customer problems are common enough to become products?
- At what point should Gintech build proprietary software?

These questions should be answered through experiments and real customer interactions.
