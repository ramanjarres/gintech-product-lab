# Case Study 12 — Alico

## Research Metadata

- **Organization:** Alico S.A.S. / BIC
- **Region:** Medellín, Colombia
- **Industry:** Flexible packaging manufacturing
- **Technology:** Epicor Kinetic and specialized analytical models
- **Research Focus:** Operational outcomes and economic-value measurement
- **Evidence Type:** Technology-partner case study
- **Research Status:** Secondary research documented

---

## 1. Research Question

How should Gintech evaluate whether an analytical intervention
creates measurable operational and economic value?

---

## 2. Documented Business Problem

TCP describes fragmented accounting systems and Excel-based
developments that limited operational integration and visibility.

Alico implemented Epicor Kinetic across sales, demand planning,
production, supply chain and distribution.

Source: [1]

---

## 3. Reported Inventory Result

TCP reports that a statistical model, developed using 18 months
of ERP information, enabled a 50% inventory reduction without
compromising operations.

The publication does not specify the inventory category, baseline,
measurement period, model or quantified service-level comparison.

This is a reported operational outcome, not an independently
verified ROI calculation.

Source: [1]

---

## 4. Additional Decision Pattern: Production Sequencing

TCP separately describes a custom sequencing algorithm developed
with academic support.

The ERP supplies job specifications to the algorithm, which
returns a production sequence for planning.

This illustrates an analytical layer extending an existing
business system.

The sequencing implementation should not be assumed to explain
the reported inventory reduction.

Sources: [1], [2]

---

## 5. Evidence Boundaries

The main source is a commercial technology partner.

The implementation concerns an established manufacturer.
Transferability to Gintech's initial customers remains untested.

For Gintech, the case remains Level 2 — Secondary Evidence.

---

## 6. Gintech Interpretation

### Operational improvement requires an economic explanation

A percentage change is a starting point.

A pilot must establish what changed, how it was measured,
what it is worth and what it cost to achieve.

### Inventory reduction needs service constraints

A proposed inventory pilot should measure availability,
production interruptions and fulfillment alongside inventory.

Reducing stock while increasing shortages may undermine value.

### Existing systems can support an additional intelligence layer

Gintech could investigate a narrowly defined decision that an
existing business system does not adequately support.

This is a product hypothesis. It does not establish that every ERP
customer needs an additional solution.

---

## 7. Proposed Decision Points

The following are Gintech research hypotheses.

| Decision | Required context | Potential action |
|---|---|---|
| How much inventory should be maintained? | Demand, lead times, stock accuracy and service targets | Adjust purchasing or stock policies |
| In what order should jobs run? | Materials, machine availability, setup requirements and deadlines | Revise the production schedule |
| When should capacity be expanded? | Demand scenarios, utilization and equipment lead times | Evaluate an investment |

The first pilot should select one decision and one responsible owner.

---

## 8. Proposed Value Measurement

This framework is a Gintech proposal, not a published Alico calculation.

| Dimension | What to measure |
|---|---|
| Inventory | Average stock value at a consistent valuation basis |
| Service | Stockouts, fulfillment and on-time delivery |
| Operations | Production interruptions, setup time and emergency purchases |
| Recurring benefit | Avoided storage, financing, waste or operational costs |
| Investment | Diagnosis, implementation, integration and training |
| Ongoing cost | Software, infrastructure, support and maintenance |

### Separate cash release from recurring savings

A reduction in inventory may release working capital.

That cash effect should be recorded separately from recurring
cost savings and accounting profit.

Do not count the same benefit twice.

### Proposed calculation

Net recurring annual benefit =
verified recurring annual benefits
minus recurring annual solution costs.

Simple payback =
one-time implementation investment
divided by positive net recurring annual benefit.

This is an evaluation method for a future pilot.
It is not an estimate of Alico's return.

---

## 9. Proposed Validation Design

1. Select one inventory category or production process.
2. Document the current decision rules and constraints.
3. Establish a representative baseline.
4. Check data completeness and inventory accuracy.
5. Agree on the primary outcome and service safeguards.
6. Test the recommendation with human review.
7. Record recommendations, decisions and actions.
8. Compare equivalent periods or a suitable comparison group.
9. Account for demand, seasonality, prices and other process changes.
10. Calculate benefits and total delivery costs.
11. Decide whether to continue, revise or stop.

---

## 10. Product Hypothesis

> Manufacturers or distributors with reliable operational records
> may pay for intelligence that improves one recurring planning
> decision while preserving agreed service levels.

This remains unvalidated.

The initial diagnostic should determine whether the opportunity
is valuable, technically feasible and accessible to Gintech.

---

## 11. Research Gaps

- What inventory was included in the reported reduction?
- Was inventory measured in units, value or days of coverage?
- What baseline and comparison period were used?
- What decisions changed after the model was introduced?
- Were service levels measured consistently?
- What other operational changes occurred?
- What were implementation and maintenance costs?
- How much benefit came from integration versus analysis?
- Can a similar decision be tested in an accessible customer?
- Can Gintech deliver sufficient value without extensive customization?

---

## 12. Research Conclusion

For Gintech, this case motivates evaluating analytical work through
operational outcomes, service constraints and economic effects.

The next task is to validate a comparable decision with potential
customers and accessible data.

A reported improvement in an external case does not establish
Gintech's market demand or expected return.
