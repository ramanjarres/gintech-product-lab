# Case Study: Nestlé Sell-In & Sell-Out Demand Forecasting

## Source

Company: Nestlé Chile
Technology Partner: Amazon Machine Learning Solutions Lab
Domain: Demand Intelligence / Retail / Supply Chain
Year: 2024
Use Case: Sell-in and sell-out demand forecasting

Source:
Enhancing Sell-In and Sell-Out Forecasting Using Ensemble Machine Learning Method

---

## Business Problem

Nestlé manages hundreds of products across the different geographical
markets where it operates.

Each product generates demand data that needs to be forecasted at
weekly and monthly levels.

These forecasts support important business processes including:

- Demand planning
- Inventory management
- Production planning
- Supply chain planning
- Financial planning

The challenge is that different products have different demand patterns,
seasonality and life cycles.

New products are also introduced while existing products can be
modified or discontinued.

This makes forecasting demand at scale a complex business problem.

---

## Sell-In vs Sell-Out

The project distinguishes between two types of demand.

### Sell-In

Demand from retailers or distributors for Nestlé products.

Example:

Nestlé
   ↓
Supermarket / Distributor

The question is:

How much product will retailers or distributors order?

### Sell-Out

Demand generated by final consumers.

Example:

Supermarket
   ↓
Consumer

The question is:

How much product will consumers actually purchase?

Both forecasts are important because they represent different stages
of the supply chain.

---

## Existing Approach

Before the machine learning solution, Nestlé Chile used traditional
time-series forecasting techniques such as exponential smoothing.

These forecasts were also adjusted using:

- Business knowledge
- Demand planner experience
- Expert judgment

While this approach provided valuable domain knowledge, it introduced
subjectivity and was difficult to scale across hundreds of products.

---

## Business Objective

Develop a scalable forecasting system capable of improving demand
forecast accuracy across multiple products and product categories.

The system needed to support existing demand planning processes rather
than replace them.

---

## Data

Nestlé maintains historical time-series data for its products.

The data can exist at different levels including:

- Product
- Product category
- Client
- Geographic market

The available forecasting frequencies include:

- Daily
- Weekly
- Monthly

Different business teams also require different forecasting horizons.

Examples include:

- Weekly operational planning
- Monthly demand planning
- Longer-term financial planning

---

## Key Data Challenges

Retail demand is not constant.

Different products can exhibit:

- Different seasonality patterns
- Different demand levels
- Product launches
- Product discontinuations
- Product modifications
- Changing consumer behavior

This makes maintaining an individual forecasting model for every
product difficult to scale.

---

## Machine Learning Approach

Nestlé Chile and Amazon Machine Learning Solutions Lab developed a
machine learning forecasting pipeline.

Instead of building an independent model for every individual product,
similar products were grouped into product categories.

Conceptually:

Product A
Product B
Product C
Product D
     ↓
Product Category
     ↓
Shared Forecasting Model

This allows the model to learn patterns from a larger collection of
related time series.

It also reduces the number of models that need to be maintained.

---

## DeepAR

The solution explored DeepAR, an autoregressive deep learning model
designed for time-series forecasting.

A major advantage of this approach is the ability to learn from
multiple related time series instead of treating every product as a
completely independent forecasting problem.

This makes the approach useful when forecasting large product
catalogs.

---

## XGBoost

The project also used XGBoost regression models.

XGBoost allows additional structured business information and
domain-specific variables to contribute to the prediction.

Rather than relying on a single forecasting algorithm, the project
combined different modeling approaches.

---

## Ensemble Approach

The final solution used an ensemble approach combining:

DeepAR
+
XGBoost
+
Business information

The architecture also connected sell-out and sell-in information.

Future expected consumer demand could therefore influence predictions
about current retailer or distributor demand.

Conceptually:

Consumer Demand
      ↓
Sell-Out Forecast
      ↓
Influences
      ↓
Sell-In Forecast
      ↓
Retail / Distribution Planning

This connects different stages of the demand chain instead of
forecasting them completely independently.

---

## Model Evaluation

The machine learning approach was compared against traditional
statistical forecasting benchmarks.

The reported approach outperformed the benchmark statistical models
by more than 50%.

However, forecasting accuracy alone was not the final objective.

The forecasts needed to support real business planning activities.

---

## Business Integration

The solution was designed to integrate with Nestlé's existing demand
planning environment.

Business intelligence dashboards were created so forecasting results
could be used alongside existing planning tools.

The architecture was designed to support:

- Additional products
- Removed products
- New product categories
- Different geographic markets

This made scalability an explicit product requirement.

## Business Value

More accurate demand forecasting can improve decisions related to:

- Inventory
- Production
- Distribution
- Demand planning
- Financial planning

The business value therefore comes from improving decisions made
before demand actually occurs.

## Key Findings

### 1. Forecasting is a decision-support capability

The purpose of forecasting is not simply to predict a number.

The forecast supports decisions such as:

- How much should we produce?
- How much inventory should we maintain?
- How much product will distributors require?
- What demand should financial planning expect?

---

### 2. Similar products can learn together

Instead of maintaining one model per product, related products can be
grouped.

This provides:

- More training data
- Shared demand patterns
- Fewer models
- Easier maintenance
- Better scalability

---

### 3. Sell-in and sell-out should not necessarily be isolated

Consumer demand can influence retailer and distributor demand.

Connecting these signals can provide a more complete view of the
commercial system.

---

### 4. Domain knowledge remains valuable

The previous forecasting process relied heavily on demand planners.

The machine learning approach does not imply that business knowledge
becomes irrelevant.

Instead, business information can become part of the forecasting
system.

---

### 5. Scalability must be designed from the beginning

A useful forecasting product must handle:

- New products
- Removed products
- Multiple categories
- Different markets

A successful prototype is not enough if the architecture cannot scale.

---

### 6. Forecasts need to integrate into existing workflows

Nestlé connected forecasting outputs with business intelligence and
existing demand planning processes.

The forecast therefore becomes part of a business workflow rather
than an isolated machine learning experiment.
