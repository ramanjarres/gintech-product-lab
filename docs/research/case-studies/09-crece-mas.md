# Case Study 09 — Crece Más

## Research Metadata

- **Organization:** Crece Más
- **Region:** Latin America — Peru, Chile and Colombia
- **Domain:** Traditional retail and supply-chain digitalization
- **Opportunity Areas:** Operations Intelligence / Demand Intelligence
- **Research Focus:** Technology adoption in micro and small businesses
- **Research Status:** Secondary research documented; validation gaps remain
- **Evidence Type:** Company website, university project description and technology-provider case study

---

## 1. Research Question

Can a platform connect small retailers, distributors and consumers,
and turn operational data into information that supports recurring
business decisions?

This case investigates how operational digitalization and analytics
can be delivered to businesses with limited technical resources.

It does not establish product-market fit for Gintech.

---

## 2. Business Context

Crece Más presents itself as a platform connecting three groups:

- Distributors and brands
- Small retailers
- Consumers

Its stated purpose includes improving traditional-channel efficiency
and supporting microentrepreneurs.

The company website describes separate offerings for distributors,
stores and consumers.

The PUCP documented a joint project with Grupo Facele in December 2021.

Sources: [1], [2], [3]

---

## 3. Business Problem

The PUCP project description identifies two related problems:

### Small retailers

Limited access to digital management tools and business practices
that support their operations.

### Brands and distributors

Limited integration with retailers, making it difficult to understand
customers and product behavior throughout the distribution channel.

These problems suggest that access to intelligence depends partly on
how business transactions are captured and connected.

Source: [2]

---

## 4. Users and Potential Buyers

| Participant | Role in the platform |
|---|---|
| Retailer | Manages business operations and places supplier orders |
| Distributor or brand | Connects with retailers and manages commercial interactions |
| Consumer | Orders from participating nearby stores |

The public website targets both retailers and distributors.

However, the reviewed sources do not establish:

- Who pays for each service
- Whether retailers receive subsidized access
- Pricing or contract structure
- Which participant owns the purchasing decision

Users, beneficiaries and paying customers should therefore be
investigated separately.

Source: [1]

---

## 5. Data

The documented platform functions involve:

- Sales
- Products and inventory
- Customer and supplier orders
- Electronic invoicing
- Customer records

The company also advertises geographically referenced consumption
trends and collection management.

The public material does not provide a complete data model,
integration specification or data-quality assessment.

Sources: [1], [2], [3]

---

## 6. Approach

The platform combines operational management with access to
business information.

The PUCP describes modules for:

- Consumer orders
- Retailer operations
- Distributor interactions
- Tax and financial management

Its description also includes an educational component for
microentrepreneurs.

AWS reports the use of Amazon EC2, Amazon RDS, Amazon QuickSight
and Amazon OpenSearch Service.

The AWS case describes real-time sales and inventory information,
including visibility into top-selling products.

Sources: [2], [3]

### Technical Evidence Boundary

The company advertises order automation and demand anticipation.

However, the reviewed sources do not document a specific forecasting
model, machine-learning method or predictive-performance evaluation.

Demand anticipation should be recorded as an advertised capability,
rather than a verified predictive-model result.

Source: [1]

---

## 7. Decision Point

Potential recurring decisions supported by the platform include:

| Business question | Decision | Operational action |
|---|---|---|
| Which products are selling most? | Which products deserve attention? | Review assortment and stock |
| What inventory is available? | What should be replenished? | Place a supplier order |
| What do customers purchase? | Which offer could be relevant? | Select a promotion |
| Which orders are pending? | What should be handled next? | Fulfill or follow up on orders |

This table is a Gintech interpretation of the documented functions.

The sources do not demonstrate that every decision is generated
automatically or delivered as an explicit recommendation.

---

## 8. Reported Results

AWS reports:

| Metric | Reported result |
|---|---|
| Clients joining the application | 45% growth in six months |
| Sales for businesses using the platform | Increases of up to 45% |
| Operational costs for small businesses | 25% reduction |

These metrics describe different outcomes.

Growth in the platform's customer base should not be treated as
growth in an individual retailer's sales.

The public case does not disclose the sample, baseline, measurement
method or causal attribution needed to independently verify the
business-impact figures.

Source: [3]

## 9. Evidence Quality

| Source | Contribution | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Crece Más website | Product positioning and advertised functions | Company marketing material |
| PUCP project description | Development context, modules and collaboration | PUCP participated in the project; no quantitative impact evaluation |
| AWS case study | Infrastructure and reported business results | Technology-provider case; no public measurement methodology |

This remains Level 2 — Secondary Evidence in the Gintech research

## 10. Limitations

The reviewed material does not establish:

- Active users or retention
- Adoption by country and business segment
- Pricing and willingness to pay
- Implementation and support costs
- Manual versus automated data capture
- Data completeness and accuracy
- Measured forecast quality
- Frequency of decisions influenced by the platform
- Independently verified financial returns
- How much improvement comes from digitalization versus analytics

### Alicorp Relationship

The company material supplied for this research states that Alicorp
became its first customer in February 2021.

An independent public confirmation of the relationship was not found
during this review.

Alicorp also operates a platform called Crecemos Juntos.
Its results should not be attributed to Crece Más without evidence
connecting the specific implementation and outcomes.

Source: [4]

---

## 11. Gintech Relevance

### Operational capture may precede intelligence

This case introduces a qualification to the thesis that businesses
already possess usable data.

Some businesses may first need more consistent digital records
before analytics can support reliable decisions.

The initial diagnosis should distinguish between:

1. Existing usable data
2. Existing fragmented or unreliable data
3. Operations that are not consistently recorded

### Intelligence can be embedded in daily work

The relevant opportunity is connecting information to recurring
tasks such as replenishment, purchasing and order management.

Gintech should investigate how a recommendation reaches the person
who can act on it.

### The buyer may differ from the user

A retailer may use the product while a distributor or brand obtains
additional value from channel visibility.

This creates a research question about distribution and monetization.

It does not establish that Gintech should adopt the same model.

### Adoption requires investigation

The educational component suggests that providing software may be
only one part of implementation.

Onboarding, business practices and continued use should be included
in future customer discovery.

---

## 12. Product Hypothesis

A potential hypothesis for Gintech is:

> Small retailers or distributors may benefit from intelligence
> that helps them decide which products to replenish, where and when,
> using available sales, inventory and order data.

This hypothesis remains unvalidated.

Required conditions include:

- An accessible target customer
- A recurring and costly decision
- Sufficiently reliable data
- A person responsible for taking action
- A measurable baseline
- A plausible paying customer

---

## 13. Validation Questions

1. Who experiences the problem most directly?
2. Who owns the decision and budget?
3. How is replenishment currently planned?
4. Which records already exist?
5. How reliable are stock and sales records?
6. What is the cost of a poor replenishment decision?
7. What tools or workarounds are used today?
8. Would the business share anonymized data for a diagnostic?
9. What would make a recommendation useful and trustworthy?
10. What measurable improvement would justify paying?

---

## 14. Research Conclusion

Crece Más provides a relevant regional example of operational
digitalization combined with business information for small retailers.

It supports investigating the relationship between data capture,
channel integration and recurring operational decisions.

The available evidence does not establish independently verified ROI,
the commercial model or demand for a Gintech solution.

The main research implication is to test one repeatable decision
with accessible customers and real data before defining an MVP.
