# Case Study 10 — TrackApp

## Research Metadata

- **Organization:** TrackApp — research pseudonym
- **Region:** Greece
- **Domain:** Retail analytics and digital products
- **Research Focus:** Connecting analytical signals to decisions and action
- **Evidence Type:** Academic single-case study
- **Research Status:** Secondary research documented

---

## 1. Research Question

How can Gintech connect an analytical signal to a decision,
an operational action and a measurable outcome?

---

## 2. Documented Case

The study examines a Greek startup adapting during COVID-19.

Its original platform combined location-based rewards with
analytics for retailers and suppliers.

| Stage | Signal or problem | Product response |
|---|---|---|
| First adaptation | Falling activity and revenue risk | Queue monitoring |
| Second adaptation | Initial recovery insufficient for profitability | Indoor tracking SDK, charged by sessions |
| Third adaptation | Declining engagement | Personalized promotions, combining location, POS and loyalty data |

The study reports qualitative improvements in engagement and
new funding, rather than a quantified causal ROI evaluation.

Source: [1], sections 3–4.

---

## 3. Evidence Boundaries

This is one qualitative case. The researchers also consulted
for the company.

It does not establish:

- Applicability to typical Colombian SMEs
- Sustainable long-term profitability
- Autonomous business decision-making
- Financial impact attributable to analytics alone

For Gintech, this remains Level 2 — Secondary Evidence.

Source: [1], methodology and limitations.

---

## 4. Gintech Interpretation: Two Decision Layers

The following distinction is a Gintech interpretation.

### Internal product decisions

A company examines signals, evaluates alternatives and decides
how to change its offering.

Gintech could support this through diagnostics, scenario analysis
and measurement.

### Decisions supported inside a customer product

A user receives information or a recommendation while completing
a business task.

Gintech could support this by delivering intelligence within
the workflow where the customer can act.

These are different opportunities. Customer discovery must
establish which one a target buyer actually needs.

---

## 5. Proposed Decision Record

For future Gintech experiments, document each decision using:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Signal | What changed? |
| Reliability | Could missing data or measurement errors explain it? |
| Business context | Why does this matter now? |
| Impact | What cost, revenue or service outcome is exposed? |
| Alternatives | What actions are realistically available? |
| Recommendation | Which action should be tested, and why? |
| Owner | Who can approve and execute it? |
| Constraints | What limits the action? |
| Measurement | How will improvement be assessed? |
| Review | When should the decision be reconsidered? |

This is a proposed Gintech framework, not an architecture
documented in TrackApp.

---

## 6. Business Metrics

For an initial Gintech pilot, distinguish three levels:

### Technical performance

Is the analysis sufficiently reliable for the decision?

### Adoption and action

Does the responsible person understand the output and act on it?

### Business outcome

Does the action improve the agreed operational or financial metric?

A pilot should define its primary outcome before implementation.

Usage, dashboard visits and recommendation acceptance can help
explain adoption, but should not automatically be treated as
financial value.

---

## 7. Product Hypothesis

> A business may benefit from intelligence that identifies a
> meaningful operational change, explains its potential impact
> and helps a responsible person select and test a response.

This is a research hypothesis, not a validated value proposition.

A first experiment should focus on one recurring decision,
with an accessible owner and an observable outcome.

---

## 8. Proposed Experiment

1. Select one recurring business decision.
2. Document the current process and baseline.
3. Check the available data and its reliability.
4. Identify one signal that could change the decision.
5. Present the signal with context and feasible alternatives.
6. Record the human decision and resulting action.
7. Measure the outcome over an agreed period.
8. Investigate other factors that could explain the change.
9. Decide whether to continue, revise or stop.

Begin with the simplest analytical method sufficient to test
the decision hypothesis.

---

## 9. Validation Questions

- What decision is repeatedly delayed or poorly informed?
- What signals are currently overlooked?
- Who has authority to act?
- What alternatives can that person realistically choose?
- What information would change the current decision?
- What happens when the recommendation is incorrect?
- How quickly must the business respond?
- What outcome would justify continued use?
- Can improvement be measured against a credible baseline?
- Would the customer commit time, data or payment to a pilot?

---

## 10. Research Conclusion

For Gintech, this case motivates documenting the connection
between evidence, human judgment, action and outcome.

The next validation task is to identify a comparable recurring
decision among accessible customers.

The case should not be used to justify building a broad
recommendation platform before that decision is validated.
